## RayMol 1.12.0 (draft)

**Materials.** Colour says what something is; a material says what it's made of. Every cartoon, surface, stick and sphere layer can now be **matte, plastic, metallic, glass, frosted glass, jelly, marble, clay or rubber**, per object. For example, a glass surface over a marble cartoon, with metallic sticks, all in one object. Your colours stay exactly as they are.

- **Pick one from the Inspector.** Each style-layer row has a *Material* dropdown. When the material has a look, a *Look* chip beside it applies the full look: marble and clay with the lighting that flatters them, and (from metallic) copper, gold, steel and chrome.
- **Or set it from the command line:** `set surface_material, glass, myprotein`. A misspelled name is an error rather than a silent fallback, and `get` shows the name back.
- **Glass that reads as one skin.** Glass-family materials peel automatically, keeping only the nearest transparent layer of the object, so a glass surface or glass sticks read as one shell. For a translucent ball-and-stick (made with `stick_transparency`), turn it on yourself with `set transparency_peel, 1, obj` and the joins inside every stick disappear.
- **Reflections.** Plastic, metallic and the glass rim reflect the background, a studio, or nothing (`material_env`). With Metal ray tracing on, they also reflect the structure itself.
- **The classic `ray` command** maps materials to its own textures and highlights. Marble traces with a swirl, clay and rubber with a matte grain, and metallic with a tinted highlight.
- **Saved with your work.** Materials are saved in scenes and sessions.
- **`default` is unchanged.** It renders exactly as before, so existing sessions look the same.

See [docs/materials.md](../materials.md) for every setting, what each material does on each representation, and the known limits.

Built on the open-source PyMOL engine. Updates install automatically via the in-app updater (**Check for Updates…** in the app menu).
